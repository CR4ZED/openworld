# Reference: everyday street life in Indian colonies

What makes an Indian street feel real, for designing the 3D world and its people. Like the road reference,
this is **general background, not data**: where a temple, garbage heap or stall actually is comes only from
the owner's survey (places pinned with "+ Place", street fields like garbage and stray animals).

## Garbage and potholes

- Open dumping is widespread. Studies report garbage heaps on open land and on streets and open spaces,
  sometimes encroaching on roads (Jaipur study, Gyan Vihar journal:
  https://www.gyanvihar.org/journals/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/ijctm-isu-2-67-to-73.pdf).
- A national review notes that disposal is mainly open dumping in a large share of urban local bodies and that
  overflowing community bins with waste around them are common (NSWAI:
  https://www.nswai.org/docs/Solid%20waste%20management%20-%20A%20knotty%20problem%20for%20urban%20India.pdf).
- Waste is often burnt in the open, so smoke near heaps is realistic (IISc journal review:
  https://journal.iisc.ac.in/index.php/iisc/article/download/4948/5214).
- Game use: litter density and heaps only where the survey says so ("garbage" field, "garbage dump spot" pins).
  Potholes from the survey's "potholes" field; water-logging puddles after rain where marked.

## Temples and roadside shrines

- Small wayside shrines are everywhere in Indian cities, often more visible than large temples; they are found
  on pavements, road edges and under sacred trees such as peepal (ECSAS panel:
  https://nomadit.co.uk/conference/ecsas2016/p/3768; LensCulture: https://www.lensculture.com/projects/36388-shrine).
- Game use: pinned temples and roadside shrines are placed exactly where surveyed, built respectfully, and
  never used in crime or violent scenes (marked sacred in data).

## Muggulu at doorsteps (Andhra-specific)

- In Andhra Pradesh, women draw muggulu (floor patterns in rice flour) at the house entrance every morning
  before dawn; it is wiped and redrawn daily, with elaborate designs at Sankranti
  (Britannica: https://www.britannica.com/art/rangoli; Wikipedia, Kolam: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolam).
- Game use: fresh muggulu at house entrances in the morning, fading through the day; large designs during
  Sankranti (January). A strong local signature that players from the area will recognise.

## Stray animals

- India had about 50 lakh (5 million) stray cattle in the 2019 Livestock Census, down from about 53 lakh in 2012
  (Factly: https://factly.in/data-the-number-of-stray-cattle-decreased-at-the-national-level-while-it-increased-in-certain-states).
- Stray dogs are common in every locality; street food waste sustains them
  (Deccan Chronicle via PressReader: https://www.pressreader.com/india/deccan-chronicle/20141104/281526519333279).
- Game use: dogs and cattle only at the density the survey's "stray animals" field gives for each street;
  cattle that block traffic and dogs near food stalls and garbage.
